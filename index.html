Predictive analytics · MLB

2027 Bounce-Back & Regression

Which hitters and pitchers are primed to rebound or fall off next year? The signal: when a player's actual results (ERA, wOBA) drift far from their expected stats (xERA, xwOBA) — the versions that strip out luck — they tend to snap back toward the expected mark. Validated on six seasons (2021–26), then applied to 2026 to project 2027.

Does the thesis hold? — Pitchers (578 season-pairs, ≥300 BF)

Yes. xERA predicts next year's ERA better than ERA itself, and the actual-minus-expected gap predicts the next-year change at r = −0.49. Bucket 2026 pitchers by how lucky they were and the next-year effect is monotonic:

Next-year ERA change, by 2026 luck

Pitchers in five buckets by their 2026 ERA−xERA gap. Lucky arms (ERA under xERA) got worse; unlucky arms bounced back.

Hitters (994 season-pairs, ≥300 PA)

Same direction, much weaker. Hitting is mostly skill, so wOBA is far stickier than ERA and there's less luck to regress — the bounce-back edge is real but small.

Read it right
This is one signal, not a projection system — it ignores age, injury, role and park, and the extreme names are often small-sample. Expected stats carry their own error. It works much better for pitchers (ERA is luck-heavy) than hitters. Think of it as "where the luck dragon visited in 2026," a prior to combine with everything else — not a point forecast.

DocChat

Retrieval-augmented generation

Upload a PDF and ask questions. DocChat retrieves the passages most relevant to your question and has Claude answer only from them, with citations — grounded in the document, not guessed.

Python · FastAPIsentence-transformersClaude APIRAG

How: the PDF is split into overlapping chunks, each embedded with a local sentence-transformers model. The question is embedded the same way and cosine similarity ranks the passages; the top matches — and only those — go to Claude with instructions to answer from them and cite each claim. Retrieval is free and local, so the app runs without an API key (showing the passages it found); a key turns on the written answers.
